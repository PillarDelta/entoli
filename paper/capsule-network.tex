\title{Mobile Agents, Second Attempt}
\subtitle{A Capsule Network of Checked, Durable, Authority-Free-in-Transit \textsc{Entoli} Agents}

\begin{abstract}
We outline a network of mobile agents over the public internet: an agent
travels as a \emph{capsule} --- its checked \textsc{Entoli} program, saved
execution state, identity metadata, and \emph{requested} permissions ---
between participating hosts, which verify it on arrival, grant permissions
under local policy, and resume it; and it interacts exclusively with
authenticated, checked peers of its own kind. The proposal is a position,
not a system: we argue it is newly feasible because its two historical
killers are now theorems in the \textsc{Entoli} design. Mobile-agent
platforms of the 1990s failed for want of (i) any checkable account of
what foreign code would do, and (ii) any account of authority captured
inside serialised state. \textsc{Entoli} supplies both: a receiving host
re-checks the program independently --- escalation beyond the granted
envelope is a compile error, not a sandbox rule --- and \emph{authority at
rest} guarantees a capsule in transit carries data but zero capability, so
interception or archival of a capsule yields no authority, and arrival
re-binds permissions under the receiver's current policy by construction.
We give the membership rule its honest form (host-enforced, never
wire-enforced; attested runtimes, for which workload-identity systems are
the building block), identify the one genuinely new mechanism required
(distributed single-ownership of a migrating continuation, cheap under
honest-but-crashing hosts, out of scope against byzantine ones), and state
what the proposal does not solve: agents still have their own goals, and
their claims still need validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{The idea, and why it died last time}

The picture is thirty years old: a program packs itself up --- code, state,
and goals --- travels to a machine closer to the data or the counterparty,
runs there, and moves on. Telescript built a language and a commercial
platform around exactly this~\cite{white1996telescript}; Aglets carried
the model to Java~\cite{lange1998aglets}. The picture failed, and the
post-mortems agree on the two reasons~\cite{chess1998mobile}. First,
\emph{the incoming-code problem}: a host could not say anything checkable
about what a visiting agent would do, so admitting one meant trusting its
author, and sandboxes of the day leaked. Second, \emph{the serialised-
authority problem}: a travelling agent's state contained its credentials
and tickets; every hop copied authority onto another machine's disk, with
no story for revocation, replay, or what a crashed hop left behind.

Both problems are now solved objects in the \textsc{Entoli}
design~\cite{entoli2026}. The language was built --- for language-model
agents, which have independently revived the demand for programs that
travel, wait, and act under someone else's authority --- so that three
properties hold at compile time: a program performing an unrecoverable
external effect does not compile; a program whose continuation would
capture an unserialisable resource does not compile; and a program
reaching a tool it was never handed does not compile. Its runtime adds
two theorems with machine-checked-style developments and a crash-tested
reference interpreter: recovery of an interrupted program is selected
from the durable store alone, and \emph{authority at rest} --- no
persisted continuation contains capability material; a resumed program
holds exactly the authority granted at resume time, never at suspend
time.

Read those properties against the two killers. The incoming-code problem
becomes: the receiving host re-runs a small checker over a
content-addressed typed AST and grants only the capabilities it chooses;
what the agent can reach is then a property of the type system, not of a
sandbox's completeness. The serialised-authority problem becomes: there
is nothing in the capsule to steal, replay, or revoke, because the
capsule \emph{cannot} contain authority --- the state carries effect-name
slots, and only the receiving host's grant table turns a slot back into
power. The 1990s model is not being retried with better fences; the two
load-bearing walls it lacked now exist.

\section{The capsule}

A capsule is the unit that travels. Its contents, all of which exist
today as artifacts of the \textsc{Entoli} implementation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Program}: the checked \textsc{Entoli} source or typed AST,
  content-addressed (the hash is the name; every referenced definition is
  in the closure, absences included).
\item \textbf{State} $\sigma$: the durable store --- journal of effect
  intents and results with per-entry recorded delivery classes, encoded
  continuations with their verification records, data classification.
  By authority at rest, $\sigma$ contains no capability material.
\item \textbf{Manifest}: identity metadata (agent identity, origin,
  itinerary), the program's hash, and the \emph{requested} rows and
  policies --- what the agent asks to be granted, phrased as effect
  requirements, which is what an \textsc{Entoli} type already is.
\end{itemize}

Arrival is the \textsc{Entoli} restart, verbatim: verify the hash
closure; re-check the program with the local checker; decide grants
under local policy (the manifest \emph{requests}, the host decides);
install handlers --- the host's own foreign bindings behind them --- and
resume. A continuation slot whose effect the host declines is a refusal
naming the effect; the capsule waits, intact, rather than running
degraded. ``Regain permissions under local policy'' is not a feature to
build; it is what resumption already means.

\section{The membership rule, honestly}

``Interacts ONLY with its own kind'' decomposes into three parts with
three different enforcement points, and it is worth being precise about
which is which.

\paragraph{1. Verified programs — enforced by hosts, exists today.}
Every admitted agent's executable behaviour passes the checker on the
\emph{receiving} side. Verification is independent by construction: the
payload contains nothing opaque, so no trust in the sender's toolchain is
required. The empirical basis is encouraging: in a two-round adversarial
study, model-written \textsc{Entoli} never violated the semantic
restrictions in any of 58 generations that reached
checking~\cite{entoli2026}.

\paragraph{2. Authenticated peers — enforced by attestation, a building
block, not research.} Nothing on the wire can establish kind: any party
in any language can imitate \textsc{Entoli} messages, so message format
is not membership. Membership is \emph{attested runtimes}: hosts and
their runtimes carry workload identities in a trust domain ---
SPIFFE/SPIRE is the obvious substrate~\cite{spiffe} --- and agents,
which cannot hold long-lived secrets (their state persists and
authority-at-rest forbids credentials in it), are vouched for by the
attested host they currently inhabit. The honest statement of the rule:
the network cannot prevent an outsider from \emph{speaking} the
protocol; it ensures that members \emph{refuse to listen} --- every
peer channel terminates at an attested runtime or not at all.

\paragraph{3. Restricted communication — enforced by the type system,
and this is the part no sandbox matches.} The capsule receives exactly
one communication capability, $\Cap{Peer}$, and whatever else the host
grants; it can reach nothing further, not because a firewall filters
its traffic but because a program whose row mentions an ungrated effect
does not pass the checker, and at runtime the handler simply is not
there. Escalation beyond the delegated envelope is the containment
theorem of the base design. Two consequences come free. First,
\emph{peer messaging is an effect}, so messages carry delivery classes:
a witnessed at-least-once \texttt{Peer.send} gives agent-to-agent
messaging crash-safe, deduplicated semantics --- conversations survive
the death of either party's host, which no ad-hoc agent protocol we
know of provides. Second, taking the rule strictly is actually
expressible: if search or payments must be reachable, they enter the
network as \emph{service agents} --- checked \textsc{Entoli} programs
wrapping one foreign binding each, admitted and attested like any
member --- which is the lingua-franca conclusion of the base
paper~\cite{entoli2026} made literal.

\section{Migration}

\paragraph{In transit, a capsule is powerless.} This is the design's
signature property applied to the network: interception, copying, or
indefinite archival of a capsule in flight yields its data (whose
handling class the capsule itself declares, computed as the join of
what it holds) and \emph{no authority whatsoever}. There are no tickets
to steal, no tokens to replay, no revocation race: revocation is the
receiving host not granting. Transit security therefore reduces to
confidentiality and integrity of bytes --- ordinary problems with
ordinary solutions.

\paragraph{One active owner — the one genuinely new mechanism.}
\textsc{Entoli}'s affinity machinery (one-shot continuations, one-shot
materialisation) is per-host; nothing in it stops two hosts from
resuming two copies of a shipped $\sigma$, after which two agents act
in the world under one identity. The network needs distributed
single-ownership. Under the membership trust model --- attested,
honest-but-crashing hosts --- the mechanism is small: before shipping,
the sender durably commits a \emph{handoff record} (``migrated to host
$H$, capsule hash $h$'') as a terminal journal entry, after which its
own runtime refuses local resumption; the receiver acknowledges
admission; a crash between those steps leaves the capsule recoverable
at exactly one of the two, decidable from the two stores --- the same
frontier discipline the single-host machine already proves sound.
Against \emph{byzantine} members --- a host that lies about having
shipped --- single-ownership requires a lease registry or consensus
among hosts, and we scope that out of version one explicitly: the
membership that attestation establishes is also the membership the
handoff protocol trusts. Hierarchical continuation identity, already
mandated by the base design for concurrency, gives capsules globally
scopable names for free.

\section{Transport}

The capsule layer is deliberately transport-agnostic: TLS between
attested endpoints suffices. A self-destructing executable-envelope
transport such as CTP (capsules that run, render, wipe, and cease)
composes naturally as the wire --- it moves bytes with strong
ephemerality properties and says nothing about what the payload does,
which is precisely the half \textsc{Entoli} supplies. Two boundaries
must be kept sharp if that pairing is pursued. Ephemerality applies to
\emph{transit only}: an \textsc{Entoli} capsule's $\sigma$ must persist
durably inside the admitting host or every durability guarantee is
forfeit. And the network's security argument must not inherit the
transport's cryptographic novelty claims; the design above assumes
nothing stronger than authenticated, confidential channels.

\section{Threat model and non-goals}

Enforced: a capsule cannot act beyond its grants (containment); cannot
carry authority between hosts (authority at rest); cannot be silently
re-run against changed code or semantics (content addressing,
compatibility tokens, quarantine); cannot lose or duplicate an external
effect beyond its declared delivery contract (recovery determinacy,
conditional on contracts as always); and cannot be duplicated across
honest hosts (handoff). Assumed: host integrity within the attested
membership --- a compromised member host can misuse exactly the
authority it would legitimately hold, no more, but for the capsules it
hosts that is full misuse; attestation shrinks this surface and cannot
remove it. Not addressed, deliberately: agents with different goals
remain agents with different goals --- membership makes interaction
\emph{well-formed}, not benign; claims exchanged between agents still
need validation; and wire-level imitation by non-members is contained,
not prevented. The precise promise is the one we can keep: \emph{an
agent can move between approved hosts, regain permissions under local
policy, and interact exclusively with authenticated, checked
\textsc{Entoli} peers.}

\section{What exists and what must be built}

Exists, with proofs or tests, in the base artifact: the checker and its
receiver-side independence; the durable machine with crash-tested
recovery; authority at rest with byte-level verification; content
addressing with drift quarantine; run-from-source hosting of checked
programs. To build, in order: (1) the capsule envelope --- manifest
format and the $\sigma$ packaging, the base design's named
``interchange obligation one''; (2) host admission --- verify, re-check,
policy, grant, resume, which composes existing pieces; (3) the
\texttt{Peer} effect and host-mediated channels; (4) the handoff
protocol; (5) attestation wiring. Items (1)--(3) sit directly on the
implementation as it stands; (4) is small under the stated trust model;
(5) is integration. The appropriate next artifact is a design note and
its adversarial review, in the discipline the base project has used
throughout --- not code.

\section{Related work}

Telescript~\cite{white1996telescript} and Aglets~\cite{lange1998aglets}
define the ambition; the mobile-agent security literature
\cite{chess1998mobile} defines the failure modes this proposal is built
against. Workload identity and attestation
(SPIFFE/SPIRE~\cite{spiffe}) supply membership. Capability-based agent
confinement in live processes is established
\cite{odersky2026securing,debenedetti2025camel}; the capsule network is
what such confinement becomes when the process is allowed to die and
move. Durable execution \cite{burckhardt2021durable} supplies the
recovery vocabulary the capsule's journal carries with it. The base
design, its theorems, its reference interpreter, and the authorship
study this proposal leans on are presented in \cite{entoli2026}.

\section{Conclusion}

Mobile agents were a good idea missing two theorems. With checkable
behaviour at admission and authority that cannot exist at rest, a
capsule network over attested hosts stops being a trust exercise and
becomes an engineering plan with a short, explicit build list --- and
one honest open mechanism, distributed ownership, scoped to a trust
model attestation already pays for. The agents the network would carry
are being written by models today; the study in the base paper suggests
they can be written \emph{in} the one language whose rules make them
safe to admit.
