Mobile Agents, Second Attempt

A Capsule Network of Checked, Durable, Authority-Free-in-Transit Entoli Agents

Kyriakos [surname]

Abstract

We outline a network of mobile agents over the public internet: an agent travels as a capsule — its checked Entoli program, saved execution state, identity metadata, and requested permissions — between participating hosts, which verify it on arrival, grant permissions under local policy, and resume it; and it interacts exclusively with authenticated, checked peers of its own kind. The proposal is a position, not a system: we argue it is newly feasible because its two historical killers are now theorems in the Entoli design. Mobile-agent platforms of the 1990s failed for want of (i) any checkable account of what foreign code would do, and (ii) any account of authority captured inside serialised state. Entoli supplies both: a receiving host re-checks the program independently — escalation beyond the granted envelope is a compile error, not a sandbox rule — and authority at rest guarantees a capsule in transit carries data but zero capability, so interception or archival of a capsule yields no authority, and arrival re-binds permissions under the receiver’s current policy by construction. We give the membership rule its honest form (host-enforced, never wire-enforced; attested runtimes, for which workload-identity systems are the building block), identify the one genuinely new mechanism required (distributed single-ownership of a migrating continuation, cheap under honest-but-crashing hosts, out of scope against byzantine ones), and state what the proposal does not solve: agents still have their own goals, and their claims still need validation.

The idea, and why it died last time

The picture is thirty years old: a program packs itself up — code, state, and goals — travels to a machine closer to the data or the counterparty, runs there, and moves on. Telescript built a language and a commercial platform around exactly this (White 1996); Aglets carried the model to Java (Lange and Oshima 1998). The picture failed, and the post-mortems agree on the two reasons (Chess 1998). First, the incoming-code problem: a host could not say anything checkable about what a visiting agent would do, so admitting one meant trusting its author, and sandboxes of the day leaked. Second, the serialised- authority problem: a travelling agent’s state contained its credentials and tickets; every hop copied authority onto another machine’s disk, with no story for revocation, replay, or what a crashed hop left behind.

Both problems are now solved objects in the Entoli design (Kyriakos 2026). The language was built — for language-model agents, which have independently revived the demand for programs that travel, wait, and act under someone else’s authority — so that three properties hold at compile time: a program performing an unrecoverable external effect does not compile; a program whose continuation would capture an unserialisable resource does not compile; and a program reaching a tool it was never handed does not compile. Its runtime adds two theorems with machine-checked-style developments and a crash-tested reference interpreter: recovery of an interrupted program is selected from the durable store alone, and authority at rest — no persisted continuation contains capability material; a resumed program holds exactly the authority granted at resume time, never at suspend time.

Read those properties against the two killers. The incoming-code problem becomes: the receiving host re-runs a small checker over a content-addressed typed AST and grants only the capabilities it chooses; what the agent can reach is then a property of the type system, not of a sandbox’s completeness. The serialised-authority problem becomes: there is nothing in the capsule to steal, replay, or revoke, because the capsule cannot contain authority — the state carries effect-name slots, and only the receiving host’s grant table turns a slot back into power. The 1990s model is not being retried with better fences; the two load-bearing walls it lacked now exist.

The capsule

A capsule is the unit that travels. Its contents, all of which exist today as artifacts of the Entoli implementation:

  • Program: the checked Entoli source or typed AST, content-addressed (the hash is the name; every referenced definition is in the closure, absences included).

  • State σ\sigma: the durable store — journal of effect intents and results with per-entry recorded delivery classes, encoded continuations with their verification records, data classification. By authority at rest, σ\sigma contains no capability material.

  • Manifest: identity metadata (agent identity, origin, itinerary), the program’s hash, and the requested rows and policies — what the agent asks to be granted, phrased as effect requirements, which is what an Entoli type already is.

Arrival is the Entoli restart, verbatim: verify the hash closure; re-check the program with the local checker; decide grants under local policy (the manifest requests, the host decides); install handlers — the host’s own foreign bindings behind them — and resume. A continuation slot whose effect the host declines is a refusal naming the effect; the capsule waits, intact, rather than running degraded. “Regain permissions under local policy” is not a feature to build; it is what resumption already means.

The membership rule, honestly

“Interacts ONLY with its own kind” decomposes into three parts with three different enforcement points, and it is worth being precise about which is which.

1. Verified programs — enforced by hosts, exists today.

Every admitted agent’s executable behaviour passes the checker on the receiving side. Verification is independent by construction: the payload contains nothing opaque, so no trust in the sender’s toolchain is required. The empirical basis is encouraging: in a two-round adversarial study, model-written Entoli never violated the semantic restrictions in any of 58 generations that reached checking (Kyriakos 2026).

2. Authenticated peers — enforced by attestation, a building block, not research.

Nothing on the wire can establish kind: any party in any language can imitate Entoli messages, so message format is not membership. Membership is attested runtimes: hosts and their runtimes carry workload identities in a trust domain — SPIFFE/SPIRE is the obvious substrate (SPIFFE Project 2024) — and agents, which cannot hold long-lived secrets (their state persists and authority-at-rest forbids credentials in it), are vouched for by the attested host they currently inhabit. The honest statement of the rule: the network cannot prevent an outsider from speaking the protocol; it ensures that members refuse to listen — every peer channel terminates at an attested runtime or not at all.

3. Restricted communication — enforced by the type system, and this is the part no sandbox matches.

The capsule receives exactly one communication capability, 𝖢𝖺𝗉[Peer]\mathsf{Cap}[Peer], and whatever else the host grants; it can reach nothing further, not because a firewall filters its traffic but because a program whose row mentions an ungrated effect does not pass the checker, and at runtime the handler simply is not there. Escalation beyond the delegated envelope is the containment theorem of the base design. Two consequences come free. First, peer messaging is an effect, so messages carry delivery classes: a witnessed at-least-once Peer.send gives agent-to-agent messaging crash-safe, deduplicated semantics — conversations survive the death of either party’s host, which no ad-hoc agent protocol we know of provides. Second, taking the rule strictly is actually expressible: if search or payments must be reachable, they enter the network as service agents — checked Entoli programs wrapping one foreign binding each, admitted and attested like any member — which is the lingua-franca conclusion of the base paper (Kyriakos 2026) made literal.

Migration

In transit, a capsule is powerless.

This is the design’s signature property applied to the network: interception, copying, or indefinite archival of a capsule in flight yields its data (whose handling class the capsule itself declares, computed as the join of what it holds) and no authority whatsoever. There are no tickets to steal, no tokens to replay, no revocation race: revocation is the receiving host not granting. Transit security therefore reduces to confidentiality and integrity of bytes — ordinary problems with ordinary solutions.

One active owner — the one genuinely new mechanism.

Entoli’s affinity machinery (one-shot continuations, one-shot materialisation) is per-host; nothing in it stops two hosts from resuming two copies of a shipped σ\sigma, after which two agents act in the world under one identity. The network needs distributed single-ownership. Under the membership trust model — attested, honest-but-crashing hosts — the mechanism is small: before shipping, the sender durably commits a handoff record (“migrated to host HH, capsule hash hh”) as a terminal journal entry, after which its own runtime refuses local resumption; the receiver acknowledges admission; a crash between those steps leaves the capsule recoverable at exactly one of the two, decidable from the two stores — the same frontier discipline the single-host machine already proves sound. Against byzantine members — a host that lies about having shipped — single-ownership requires a lease registry or consensus among hosts, and we scope that out of version one explicitly: the membership that attestation establishes is also the membership the handoff protocol trusts. Hierarchical continuation identity, already mandated by the base design for concurrency, gives capsules globally scopable names for free.

Transport

The capsule layer is deliberately transport-agnostic: TLS between attested endpoints suffices. A self-destructing executable-envelope transport such as CTP (capsules that run, render, wipe, and cease) composes naturally as the wire — it moves bytes with strong ephemerality properties and says nothing about what the payload does, which is precisely the half Entoli supplies. Two boundaries must be kept sharp if that pairing is pursued. Ephemerality applies to transit only: an Entoli capsule’s σ\sigma must persist durably inside the admitting host or every durability guarantee is forfeit. And the network’s security argument must not inherit the transport’s cryptographic novelty claims; the design above assumes nothing stronger than authenticated, confidential channels.

Threat model and non-goals

Enforced: a capsule cannot act beyond its grants (containment); cannot carry authority between hosts (authority at rest); cannot be silently re-run against changed code or semantics (content addressing, compatibility tokens, quarantine); cannot lose or duplicate an external effect beyond its declared delivery contract (recovery determinacy, conditional on contracts as always); and cannot be duplicated across honest hosts (handoff). Assumed: host integrity within the attested membership — a compromised member host can misuse exactly the authority it would legitimately hold, no more, but for the capsules it hosts that is full misuse; attestation shrinks this surface and cannot remove it. Not addressed, deliberately: agents with different goals remain agents with different goals — membership makes interaction well-formed, not benign; claims exchanged between agents still need validation; and wire-level imitation by non-members is contained, not prevented. The precise promise is the one we can keep: an agent can move between approved hosts, regain permissions under local policy, and interact exclusively with authenticated, checked Entoli peers.

What exists and what must be built

Exists, with proofs or tests, in the base artifact: the checker and its receiver-side independence; the durable machine with crash-tested recovery; authority at rest with byte-level verification; content addressing with drift quarantine; run-from-source hosting of checked programs. To build, in order: (1) the capsule envelope — manifest format and the σ\sigma packaging, the base design’s named “interchange obligation one”; (2) host admission — verify, re-check, policy, grant, resume, which composes existing pieces; (3) the Peer effect and host-mediated channels; (4) the handoff protocol; (5) attestation wiring. Items (1)–(3) sit directly on the implementation as it stands; (4) is small under the stated trust model; (5) is integration. The appropriate next artifact is a design note and its adversarial review, in the discipline the base project has used throughout — not code.

Related work

Telescript (White 1996) and Aglets (Lange and Oshima 1998) define the ambition; the mobile-agent security literature (Chess 1998) defines the failure modes this proposal is built against. Workload identity and attestation (SPIFFE/SPIRE (SPIFFE Project 2024)) supply membership. Capability-based agent confinement in live processes is established (Odersky et al. 2026; Debenedetti et al. 2025); the capsule network is what such confinement becomes when the process is allowed to die and move. Durable execution (Burckhardt et al. 2021) supplies the recovery vocabulary the capsule’s journal carries with it. The base design, its theorems, its reference interpreter, and the authorship study this proposal leans on are presented in (Kyriakos 2026).

Conclusion

Mobile agents were a good idea missing two theorems. With checkable behaviour at admission and authority that cannot exist at rest, a capsule network over attested hosts stops being a trust exercise and becomes an engineering plan with a short, explicit build list — and one honest open mechanism, distributed ownership, scoped to a trust model attestation already pays for. The agents the network would carry are being written by models today; the study in the base paper suggests they can be written in the one language whose rules make them safe to admit.
